I am a postdoctoral fellow in neuroimaging at the Centre for Neuroscience Studies, Queen's University, working with Dr. DJ Cook on stroke recovery and traumatic brain injury. My research uses multimodal MRI to understand how structural and functional brain damage translates into behavioural impairment, and how the brain reorganises during recovery.

My work bridges preclinical and clinical neuroscience. In non-human primate models of middle cerebral artery occlusion, I combine longitudinal structural, diffusion and resting-state MRI with behavioural outcome scoring to characterise the trajectory of stroke recovery and the effects of rehabilitation therapy. In parallel, I study repeated subconcussive head impacts in collegiate athletes and blast exposure in military personnel using longitudinal white-matter microstructure analysis.

Before joining Queen's, I completed a PhD in Biomedical Sciences at the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, where I characterised longitudinal cortical and subcortical atrophy in Huntington's disease and functional connectivity in a parkinsonian macaque model. I continue to collaborate with UNAM on neurodegenerative disease research, particularly the spinocerebellar ataxias, and I teach in the graduate neuroscience and biomedical sciences programs there.

## Research interests

- Stroke recovery and lesion-symptom mapping
- Non-human primate models of neurological disease
- Diffusion MRI, tractography and white-matter microstructure
- Resting-state fMRI and functional connectivity
- Traumatic brain injury and repetitive head impacts
- Machine learning applied to neuroimaging and clinical outcome prediction

## Methods and tools

Multimodal MRI processing across T1w, T2w, FLAIR, DWI/DTI, resting-state fMRI and TOF angiography, in both human and non-human primate datasets. Day-to-day I work in Bash, Python, R and MATLAB on Linux, with FreeSurfer, FSL, AFNI, SPM, MRtrix3, ANTs and GRETNA.
